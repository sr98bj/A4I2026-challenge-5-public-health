## Section 4: Data Limitations & Resolution Gaps Disclosure
### 1. Ecological Fallacy & Spatial Aggregation
Census tracts in Fulton County average 4,000 residents across diverse micro-environments. Attributing tract-level uninsured rates (e.g. 28.4%) to every household within the boundary commits an ecological fallacy. Pockets of severe uninsurance can exist in moderate-income tracts, and well-insured clusters can exist in high-vulnerability tracts.

### 2. Micro-Spatial Ground-Truth vs 4-Decimal Coordinates
Geographic coordinates with 4 decimal places provide theoretical precision of approximately 11.1 meters (~36 feet). However, numerical GPS coordinates do not encode ground-truth curb realities: curb cut heights, active loading zone tow ordinances, low-hanging utility wires, generator noise ordinances, or private property towing enforcement. Ground-truthed physical scouting is essential.

### 3. Transit Schedule vs Weekend Headway Discrepancies
Published General Transit Feed Specification (GTFS) travel times assume on-time bus schedules. In practice, Saturday MARTA headways frequently experience 30-to-60 minute intervals, missed connections, and service cuts. Transit times reported here (e.g. 52-68 minutes) represent optimistic lower bounds; actual patient transit burden is frequently 1.5x to 2x greater.

### 4. Recency Gaps in ACS Data vs Real-Time Medicaid Redetermination
American Community Survey (ACS) 5-year rolling estimates lag real-time policy shocks. Specifically, Georgia's ongoing Medicaid redetermination ('unwinding') and shifts in low-income safety net qualification mean current uninsured numbers may exceed survey baselines by 15-25% in high-churn working class tracts.
